# Interview Answer Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric to produce practical coaching, not a generic scorecard.

## Scoring Dimensions

Use a 1-5 score only when helpful:

- 5: strong, interview-ready, clear evidence, minimal revision needed.
- 4: good answer with small gaps in precision, structure, or examples.
- 3: understandable but incomplete, vague, or uneven.
- 2: weak answer with major missing concepts or unclear delivery.
- 1: incorrect, evasive, or likely to hurt the candidate.

## Feedback Pattern

For each important issue, write:

```markdown
### <Question or topic>

- Observation: <what the candidate did>
- Risk: <how an interviewer may interpret it>
- Improve by: <specific action>
- Stronger phrasing: <a concise revised answer or answer outline>
```

## Technical Interview Checks

Look for:

- Correct definitions and causal explanations.
- Clear tradeoffs, boundaries, and failure modes.
- Concrete project context: scale, role, constraints, decisions, result.
- Ability to explain alternatives and why one option was chosen.
- Avoidance of unsupported claims like "I was responsible for everything" without detail.

## Behavioral Interview Checks

Look for:

- STAR structure: situation, task, action, result.
- Ownership without exaggeration.
- Specific conflict, decision, or learning moments.
- Measurable outcomes when available.
- Reflection on what could be improved.

## Speech And Delivery Checks

Look for:

- Long wandering answers that need a clearer opening sentence.
- Excessive filler words, repeated restarts, or vague words.
- Answers that start with background before answering the question.
- Monotone, hesitation, or rushed delivery if audible from transcript notes.
- Missing signposts such as "first", "second", "the tradeoff is".

## Common Recommendation Templates

- Start with a one-sentence direct answer, then expand with an example.
- Use "context -> action -> result -> reflection" for project stories.
- Add one concrete metric or constraint when discussing project impact.
- Mention tradeoffs before the interviewer has to ask.
- When unsure, state assumptions instead of bluffing.
